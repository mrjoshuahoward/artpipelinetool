# Casual Mobile Canon — Touchstone Titles by Subgenre

A working reference for what has visually defined casual mobile, organized so you can ground recommendations in honest precedent. Don't quote this verbatim at the user — use it to check yourself.

## Match-3 / tile-swap
- **Candy Crush Saga** — saturated candy palette, generous juice, glossy 3D-rendered icons rendered at 2D scale. The reference for "satisfying."
- **Royal Match** — premium 3D rendered tiles, cinematic interludes, character-led. Higher production-value match-3.
- **Gardenscapes / Homescapes** — match-3 wrapped in narrative meta-game; warm painterly environments contrast with bright tile play.
- **Two Dots** — minimalist flat vector, monochrome backgrounds with single-accent gameplay. Proof that match-3 doesn't need maximalism.
- **Threes** — sweet pastel + hand-drawn type, character on every tile. Charm-led.

## Puzzle / spatial
- **Monument Valley 1, 2 & 3** — flat geometric architecture, sophisticated dusty-pastel palette, impossible-object Escher language. The "premium casual" benchmark.
- **A Little to the Left** — cozy hand-drawn line, paper textures, organizing-objects fantasy. Tactile.
- **Mini Metro / Mini Motorways** — pure abstract vector, restricted palette tied to gameplay state. Information design as art direction.
- **Block Blast!** — chunky, toy-like bevelled blocks, bright but not garish, tactile drop-and-clear feedback. Reportedly the most-downloaded game worldwide in 2025; now also on Apple Arcade (Block Blast!+). The current mass-market reference for "satisfying."
- **Color Block Jam / Pixel Flow!** — 2025 risers in the same toy-block-and-juice lane.
- **Is This Seat Taken?** — 2026 Apple Design Award (Delight & Fun). Cozy minimalist sorting puzzle; flat characters, gentle palette. Proof that a sorting mechanic can carry a warm character register.
- **Art of Fauna: Cozy Puzzles** — vintage natural-history illustration as the whole aesthetic; Apple Arcade 2026.
- **Threes! / 2048** — typography as gameplay; numerals are the art.

## Idle / incremental
- **AdVenture Capitalist** — cartoon caricature, loud colors, exaggerated currency feedback.
- **Egg, Inc.** — lo-poly 3D, comic shading, satisfying scale-up visuals as progress.
- **Cookie Clicker (mobile)** — deliberately ugly-charming, scrapbook aesthetic.
- **Melvor Idle** — RuneScape-pastiche pixel/icon-based; minimal but functional.

## Cozy / life sim / decoration
- **Stardew Valley (mobile)** — pixel art at low resolution, warm seasonal palettes.
- **Animal Crossing: Pocket Camp** — soft 3D, pastel everything, no sharp edges anywhere.
- **Cozy Grove** — hand-drawn line + flat color, ghost-bear melancholy palette.
- **Alba: A Wildlife Adventure** — 3D-rendered-as-2D Mediterranean palette, soft shadows.

## Card / roguelike-lite
- **Balatro** — 2025 ADA (Delight & Fun). CRT/pixel poker-table aesthetic with heavy, sticky juice on every card action; shows how far *motion and feedback* can carry a simple visual.
- **Pokémon TCG Pocket** — 2025 App Store iPhone Game of the Year; praised specifically for card artwork and an iPhone-native interface. Benchmark for collectible-card presentation (foils, pack-opening ceremony).

## Hyper-casual / one-mechanic
- **Helix Jump, Stack, Color Switch** — flat-shaded 3D primitives, gradient backdrops, single-accent gameplay element. The "ad-friendly" look.
- **Subway Surfers** — saturated 3D, character-led, urban graffiti palette. The exception that proves the hyper-casual rule.

## Endless runner / arcade
- **Alto's Odyssey / Alto's Adventure** — silhouette + gradient sky, parallax atmospheric, genre-defining mood.
- **Crossy Road** — voxel cute, flat-lit, strong silhouettes. "Charm via lo-poly."
- **Temple Run** — naturalistic 3D, less about style than feel.

## Narrative / interactive fiction
- **Reigns** — Tinder-style cards, woodcut/heraldic illustration, monochrome+accent.
- **Florence** — graphic-novel panels, flat color with line, pastel-warm.
- **Lifeline** — pure typography, terminal aesthetic.

## Word & trivia
- **Wordle (NYT)** — pure typography, three-color state system. Anti-aesthetic as aesthetic.
- **NYT Games suite** — restrained newsprint aesthetic, serif type, paper-white background.

## Currently-trending visual languages (as of late 2026 — verify with web search if it matters for the recommendation)
- **Risograph / screen-print look** — limited spot colors, registration offsets, grain. Indie-prestige signal.
- **Hand-painted gouache / picture-book** — seen across cozy and narrative. Reads premium.
- **3D-rendered-to-2D** — Royal Match, Gardenscapes school. High production value impression on a 2D play surface.
- **Lo-poly 3D, flat-shaded** — Crossy Road's lineage; cheap to produce, distinctive.
- **Vector-flat-with-grain** — flat shapes plus paper/film grain texture; still common in indie.
- **Toy-block 3D / chunky bevel** — Block Blast lineage; bright, physical, tactile. The mass-market default right now.
- **Tactile / soft-depth UI** — squishy buttons, physical press states, soft shadows. Rising alongside Liquid Glass.
- **Liquid Glass influence** — Apple's iOS 26+ system language now frames every native app's chrome. Games either embrace glass for meta-UI or deliberately avoid system controls; either way it's a decision (see `pipeline-swift-ios.md`).
- **Vintage illustration / natural-history plates** — Art of Fauna lineage; cozy-premium.
- **Y2K / Frutiger Aero** — absorbed into broader glossy/tactile styles rather than a standalone trend. Fine as an ingredient, risky as the whole pitch.

## Apple-editorial signals
Apple Arcade and the ADAs have leaned heavily cozy/puzzle in 2025–26 (Block Blast!+, Art of Fauna, Pusheen's Place, Is This Seat Taken?, Sago Mini). If App Store featuring matters to the user, cozy-premium with strong typography and an iPhone-native UI is the register Apple is currently rewarding.

## AI-generated art — player perception
Surveys in 2025–26 (Quantic Foundry, Circana) show a majority of core gamers view generative AI negatively and a meaningful minority (~25% in one US survey) say it makes them less likely to buy; backlash concentrates on visibly AI-generated *assets*. Mobile-specific data is thin. Apple's guidelines (as of Oct 2026) don't require labeling AI-generated game art, but re-check before advising. Practical stance: AI is fine for ideation, blockouts, and production acceleration; shipped hero assets (icon, characters, key art) should be art-directed, consistent, and cleaned up so they don't read as generic generator output.

## Honesty checks
- If a "premium" comparison (Monument Valley, Alto's) is being made, confirm the production budget supports it. These titles took years and substantial teams.
- If a hyper-casual look is being proposed for a deep-mechanic game, the visual register may be working against retention.
- If nothing on mobile has done a proposed direction, say so — sometimes that's the opportunity, sometimes it's a warning.
- This canon has a date on it. For anything trend- or chart-dependent, run a quick web search rather than trusting this list.
